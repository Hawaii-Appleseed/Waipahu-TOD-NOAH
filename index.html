Potential naturally occurring affordable housing near Waipahu rail

Older, privately owned apartment buildings with no public subsidy around the Hōʻaeʻae (West Loch) and Pouhala (Waipahu Transit Center) rail stations. TOD zoning allows denser redevelopment here, which can raise the chance these buildings are sold or replaced.

    How these were identified

    Adapted from Song & Reid (2026), "The Implications of Naturally Occurring Affordable Housing (NOAH) Sales for Residential Mobility," Journal of the American Planning Association. A building is counted when it:

    • has 5 to 49 homes on one parcel, with its main building built before 1990;
    • has one owner for the whole building;
    • is not government-owned, not HUD-subsidized (LIHTC, project-based Section 8, public housing) and has no large property-tax exemption;
    • is not used as a hotel, group quarters or military housing.

    The area covers both adopted TOD Special Districts (Ord. 2017-054), the 2014 Waipahu Neighborhood TOD Plan area, and anything within ½ mile of either station. The rings mark ¼ and ½ mile in a straight line from each station; the city's TOD planning areas use a ½-mile radius.

    Gray dots are older condo buildings that meet every rule except single ownership. Their units are owned separately, so they are left out of the count, though some units may be rented.

    These are candidates. Rents and building condition have not been checked. Owner type comes from the owner's name on the tax roll, and "Company or LLC" includes family holding companies. Owner names are not shown. Building records date from about 2013; ownership and assessed values are from the 2026 tax roll.
